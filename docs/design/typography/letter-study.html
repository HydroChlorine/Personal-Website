01 / Upright baseline

A place to be curious.

I like the moment when a small observation opens a much bigger question. A dim blue glow, a curve that refuses to fit, or a program that behaves unexpectedly can all be reasons to look a little closer. Some questions give satisfying answers; others stay with me. This website is a place to keep both, alongside the small things I make along the way.

04 / Stress test · too much of a good thing

A little difference in a coefficient can change the effect. I like coffee and will still follow the next question.

The accents start to look like rendering mistakes and distract from meaning. This is why letter-pair rules need an editorial gate and a density limit.

05 / Technical writing · clear meaning first

More measurements do not automatically give a better answer. We need independent information, rather than another version of the same observation.

F₀ / F = 1 + KSV[Q]

Whole-phrase emphasis is semantic; letter decoration is omitted here. Formula italics should follow mathematical notation. No tilt on numbers, units, code, citations or navigation.

06 / Other tiny accents · optional alternatives

Small questions_

A static trailing underscore can belong to a banner or a terminal-like greeting. A cyan underline on a hovered link can signal interaction. Neither needs a dancing, blinking or randomly changing letter inside an article.

All specimens are proposal-only. Letter slants are synthetic obliques; the downloaded families supply regular styles. Source files, licenses, and the reasoning are recorded in TYPOGRAPHY_PROPOSAL.md.
